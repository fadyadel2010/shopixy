Expiry is not a claim that a process died: it makes an attempt eligible for **safe database fencing**. A superseded worker outside its business transaction cannot pass the latest-generation/owner check. A slow or paused worker inside its authoritative transaction retains an inbox `FOR UPDATE NOWAIT` lock; reclaim/renew/fail contenders are refused, even after expiry. Database rollback releases the lock without committing effects. An indefinitely paused database transaction deliberately sacrifices availability; no timeout-based forced takeover is implemented.

- `PointOfSale.Core/Entities/ShippingWebhookClaim.cs`: tenant-owned attempt with log ID, increasing bigint generation, UUID owner, acquisition/lease/finish timestamps, outcome and bounded error. SELECT` reclamation, conditional renewal/completion/failure; `clock_timestamp()` and a fixed **60-second eligibility lease**. ### 2. Earlier generic webhook root cause and containment — history, recovery gate retained

Actual code: `CarrierShipmentService.ProcessWebhookInternalAsync`, `PrepareShippingWebhookLogAsync`, conflict resolution and failure persistence; `ShippingWebhooksController` translates `Success/StatusCode` to HTTP. Original lifecycle commits `Processing` with `SaveChanges` **before** opening the separate serializable business-mutation transaction. A crash after that save, during mutation, after rollback, or before failure-audit persistence leaves a durable `Processing` record. Request cancellation also passes the cancelled token into failure persistence, which can fail. Both normal duplicate lookup and unique-insert-conflict handling originally returned successful duplicate200 for `Processing`, losing retry pressure even though no completion was established. Failed/Pending records were automatically reused without an atomic claim, rewriting identity/trust/failure evidence; two retries could both proceed. Failure persistence could also overwrite a genuinely Completed record after a lost commit acknowledgement.

Source containment in `PointOfSale.Infrastructure/Shipping/CarrierShipmentService.cs`:

1. Only Completed/Skipped remain successful terminal duplicates. Processing returns **503 `WEBHOOK_PROCESSING_UNRESOLVED`** in both lookup and unique-conflict paths; it is not stolen, completed, or acknowledged as success.
2. Existing Failed/Pending/other unfinished records return **409 `WEBHOOK_RECONCILIATION_REQUIRED`** without replay, identity reassignment, or audit clearing. This deliberately sacrifices unattended retry availability rather than risk replaying partially committed effects.
3. Failure audit is attempted only after the initial claim save returned successfully. It neither inserts a fictitious claim after a failed insert nor downgrades terminal evidence. Relational updates use an atomic conditional `ExecuteUpdate` fenced by persisted ID/tenant/carrier/event/hash and status Processing; synthetic InMemory fixtures exercise the guarded fallback. **The relational SQL branch has not been executed against live data.**

These corrections are **safety containment, not automatic recovery**. A 24-hour-old Processing record still cannot be called genuinely stale: an active worker may be paused, and no durable ownership/heartbeat proves otherwise. No timestamp threshold or unsupported lease fields were invented. No reconciliation endpoint, scheduler reset, manual SQL status edit, or carrier event replay was used.
